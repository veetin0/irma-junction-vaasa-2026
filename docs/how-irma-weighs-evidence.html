Irma · how the numbers are made

How Irma weighs evidence

Every percentage on Irma's overview comes from a short chain of simple arithmetic. This page walks through that chain with real examples from the data of 4 October 2026, and says plainly which parts are standard methods and which parts are our own choices.

In short
  • A signal's weight is five simple answers multiplied together: how reliable the publisher is, how fresh the news is, how big the change is, how much doubt has been found, and what the planner thinks.
  • Each weighted signal pushes the situations it fits up and the ones it contradicts down. The pushes are added up and turned into percentages that always total 100%.
  • The building blocks are well-known methods. The way they are combined and the numbers inside them are our own judgement, not an official standard, and they have not yet been checked against ABB's actual orders.

Why multiply?

Multiplying works like a chain: the weakest link decides. A huge number from an unreliable blog should count little, and so should a solid fact that is two years old. If the five answers were added instead, a top score on one question could hide a failing score on another.

One safety rule

Until Irma has searched for counter-evidence, a signal's weight is capped at 0.6. A claim that nobody has examined can never dominate the picture.

Strong and weak

A weight of 0.25 or more is listed as a strong signal, anything lower as weak. Today 10 of the 32 signals are strong. The automatic search adds a new signal only if its weight still reaches 0.15 after all its checks.

How doubts add up

The Fingrid card on signed data-centre connections is reliable (grade A) but has three pieces of counter-evidence. Their strengths are 0.40, 0.21 and 0.40. Step 2 of 3

How a signal pushes the situations

Every signal is described by six themes: demand, component supply, rules and policy, material prices, a shift toward the Nordics, and stock-building by middlemen. Each of the nine candidate situations has its own pattern on the same six themes. The better the two patterns agree, the harder the signal pushes that situation up. If they point in opposite directions, it pushes the situation down. Each theme is scored from −1 to +1. Plus means more demand, tighter components, friendlier rules, dearer materials, a shift toward the Nordics, or middlemen stockpiling.

Irma compares the two patterns theme by theme. Demand agrees strongly (0.9 × 1.0) and the Nordic shift agrees a little (0.9 × 0.3). Adding these gives 1.17, and dividing by the size of the situation's own pattern (2.6) gives a match of 0.45. The match always lands between −1 and +1. The push is then 1.5 × weight × match: 1.5 × 0.59 × 0.45 = +0.40. The 1.5 is a fixed setting that limits how far one signal can move the picture.

Standard methods or our own invention?

Both. Each building block is an established, well-understood method. The recipe that combines them, and the numbers inside it, are our own choices for this prototype.

What this means for reading the percentages

Irma's percentages are a transparent ranking of today's evidence, not measured probabilities. Every number can be opened and traced to its sources, but nobody has yet checked how often a situation shown at 50% actually happens. Until that check is done, read 50% as "today's evidence points this way most strongly", not as "a 50% chance".

How the numbers can be checked and tuned

  1. Keep the percentages over time. Irma already stores one point per day.
  2. After 6 to 18 months, compare them with what happened: ABB's order intake by segment and which situation actually unfolded.
  3. Score the past percentages with a standard accuracy measure for probability forecasts, the Brier score.
  4. Adjust the numbers we chose (grades, half-lives, the setting 1.5, starting beliefs) to the values that would have scored best, and repeat.
Figures come from Irma's data for the current world situation on 4 October 2026 and are rounded. The formulas are in the app's engine files weights.py and archetypes.py. Irma shows evidence and alternatives; it does not recommend order quantities or timing.
