# Irma demo video: voiceover script

Video length 6:33. The screen recording runs 0:21 to 6:18, then black to the end.
Read time is about 6 minutes at a calm pace. Timecodes are where each block should start.

---

**0:00 · Opening scene (ABB planners at the table)**

Is the data-centre boom here to stay, or is part of it double ordering that will unwind? That is the question ABB's planners face. The early signs are scattered across news, prices, grid reports and research. By the time they reach the order book, it's late.

**0:21 · Overview and summary**

This is Irma. Every hour, Irma reads public signals and rebuilds its picture of the market. At the top is a plain-language summary. Today, demand for power equipment from Nordic data centres is still growing, and chips and memory are still hard to get. The most likely path for the next one to three years: growth continues, components stay scarce.

**0:48 · Region, segment and signals**

The region and segment selectors show where Irma is heading. In this prototype, we focus on Nordic data centres. On the left are the signals that drive the picture, each weighted between zero and one. The heaviest today is Google's thirteen-billion-euro data-centre programme in Finland, at zero point five nine.

**1:12 · The four most likely situations**

In the centre are the four most likely situations, chosen from nine candidates. The leader, "growth continues, components stay scarce", stands at fifty percent. The chart shows an illustrative demand path against the supply that can actually be delivered, with an uncertainty band, over six months, one year or three years. It is not a sales forecast. The History tab shows how each situation's share has moved day by day.

**1:51 · Scenario detail**

Click a smaller card, and it swaps into the main view. In second place: investment concentrates in the Nordics, at thirty-six percent. Here, moratoria in Amsterdam, Dublin and Frankfurt push new capacity north. Every scenario has a full card: what would confirm it, what would refute it, and what it would mean for ABB, always stated as a condition. And every percentage can be traced. These bars show which signals pushed this scenario up, and which pushed it down.

**2:36 · Sources and market summary**

Back on the overview, the selected situation lists every source it relies on, with the publisher, the date and the weight. The market summary puts it all in one paragraph: the leading picture, how uncertain it is, and the strongest evidence for and against, linked to each signal card. It always ends the same way. The planner decides what this means for orders. Irma shows the evidence and the alternatives.

**3:10 · Assistant and alerts**

Or just ask. "Give me an update on copper busbars." Irma answers only from its signal cards, and cites each one. When no card covers the question directly, it says so, and uses the closest evidence: here, the copper price. When a major signal lands, like the Uptime Institute survey naming power as the main constraint, Irma raises an alert with its weight and source.

**3:39 · Automatic signal search**

On every update, a search agent scans trusted sources for new signals. This run found two candidates and rejected both, because Irma already had them. A new source must pass nine rules, such as a publisher graded B or better, an age of thirty days at most, and a counter-evidence check. If nothing passes, nothing is added.

**4:05 · Strong and weak signals**

The signals page separates strong signals from weak ones. Weak signals count for little today, but they can become decisive, so planners can lock the ones worth following.

**4:18 · Inside a signal**

Open any signal, and everything is on one page: the quote, the source and the date. Why it counts as structural rather than cyclical: here, a twenty-two-year nuclear power agreement. How the weight is calculated: reliability, freshness and strength, reduced by counter-evidence. Code does the arithmetic. The language model only reads and classifies. And the counter-evidence itself: the money lands in 2027 and 2028, and relay orders lag behind site announcements.

**4:50 · Research signals**

Irma also tracks research institutions, such as the Uptime Institute and the IEA. Older studies fade automatically, so a 2024 report counts for far less than this month's news. Here, the Finnish government estimates that committed data centres will add eight to ten terawatt-hours a year: about a tenth of Finland's electricity use.

**5:14 · Settings and language**

In Settings, the planner decides how often Irma updates, here every sixty minutes. They also set what counts as a large change, which signals trigger an alert, and which websites the agents may search. Every threshold is visible, and every one can be changed. And Irma works in both English and Finnish.

**5:45 · Stress test: world situation 2**

Finally, a stress test. We switch to a constructed downturn, built from demo data. Hyperscalers cut spending, the copper price falls, and components get easier to find. Irma's picture changes completely. The leader is now "double orders unwind, stock is released", at thirty-six percent, almost tied with "investment slows down", at thirty-five. Same formulas, different evidence.

**6:18 · Close (over black)**

Irma doesn't tell ABB what to buy. It turns weak signals into traceable evidence, so planners can see change coming and decide earlier. Irma: from weak signals to proactive decisions.
